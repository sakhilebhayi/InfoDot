Government buys everything from stationery to construction — and buys it under rules that are public, mechanical, and unforgiving of paperwork errors. A small business that registers properly, watches the right portals and submits complete documents competes on fair terms; one that doesn't is disqualified before price is even read. This entry is the pathway — Central Supplier Database registration, quotations versus tenders, the preference-points system as it works NOW (it changed in 2022; much online advice is wrong), the standard forms, the 30-day payment rule, and the fake-RFQ scam that targets new suppliers. One framework note: the Constitutional Court struck down the new Public Procurement Act in September 2026 before it ever commenced, so the existing PFMA/MFMA/PPPFA regime described here continues to apply — and may be legislated again, so verify against current Treasury instructions when bidding.

## The problem
Bids fail for process reasons, not price: the supplier isn't on the CSD (buyers may only invite and award quotes to CSD-registered suppliers), the tax status isn't compliant, a form is unsigned or retyped, the submission is late, or preference points are claimed under the old B-BBEE-level system that the 2022 regulations replaced — the Treasury implementation guide lists using the old B-BBEE points table as a common error that risks irregular expenditure. Separately, criminals exploit the hunger for government orders with fake RFQs that end with you delivering goods, or paying a "supplier deposit", for an order that never existed.

## Who this is for
Any registered South African business that wants public-sector customers — national or provincial departments, public entities, or municipalities.

## Step 1 — Clear the gates: CIPC, SARS, bank
You need CIPC registration, a SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) in order, and a business bank account. Tender screening checks tax compliance via your TCS PIN or your CSD record, and a non-compliant status sinks the bid regardless of merit.

## Step 2 — Register on the Central Supplier Database (free)
Registering at csd.gov.za costs nothing. You create a user account (email + SMS OTP), then capture the business's identification, industry classification, addresses, tax details, directors/members, commodities you supply, locations you serve, any accreditations (e.g. CIDB for construction) and B-BBEE information. The CSD verifies against SARS, CIPC, Home Affairs and the restricted-suppliers list; bank verification can take up to 4 days. Success issues a supplier number and unique registration reference. Two maintenance duties: every physical address needs proof no older than 3 months (a Treasury rule effective 1 August 2024 — profiles that don't comply get deactivated), and your commodity/location choices decide which RFQs ever reach you, so set them carefully.

## Step 3 — Understand the two buying lanes
- **Price quotations (RFQs)**: for national/provincial purchases above R2,000 up to R1 million (all taxes included), buyers obtain at least three quotes from CSD-registered suppliers — often by inviting suppliers selected FROM the CSD rather than advertising. Being registered, with the right commodities, IS your marketing here. Municipal bands differ (quotations up to R300,000 for local municipalities, R200,000 for districts, R750,000 for metros, per the December 2023 municipal SCM amendment), and each municipality has its own SCM policy.
- **Competitive bids (tenders)**: above R1 million nationally (above the municipal bands locally), advertised on National Treasury's eTenders portal (etenders.gov.za) and in the Government Tender Bulletin; awards are published there too. Watch both, plus the websites of departments and municipalities you can actually serve.

## Step 4 — Learn the preference-points system as it is NOW
Under the 2022 Preferential Procurement Regulations: tenders up to R50 million (taxes included) are scored 80/20 — 80 points for price, 20 for "specific goals"; above R50 million it's 90/10. The specific goals are set by each buyer in its own policy within the PPPFA's framework (ownership by race, gender, disability; RDP goals) — the B-BBEE status-level points table is no longer the mechanism. A live example of how it looks in practice: a national tender scoring up to 10 points for women ownership, 7 for black ownership, 3 for disability ownership, with evidence by CIPC certificate plus sworn affidavit or valid B-BBEE certificate. Equal scores break on the specific-goal score, then by lot.

## Step 5 — Master the standard bid documents (SBDs)
Tender packs are built from standard forms: SBD 1 (invitation and bidder details), SBD 3-series (pricing), SBD 4 (declaration of interest), SBD 6.1 (preference-points claim). The mechanical rules from live national tenders: use the issued document itself (retyping or disassembling it gets the bid rejected); sign originals in ink and initial alterations; fixed prices only (variable prices are rejected); bids typically stay valid 90 days; late bids are returned unopened; bids from persons in the service of the state — or companies with such directors — are not considered; and missing required documents may invalidate the bid. For joint ventures, EVERY party needs its own tax-compliance proof.

## Step 6 — Invoice correctly and know the 30-day rule
Treasury Regulation 8.2.3 requires national and provincial departments to pay within 30 days of receiving an invoice (unless the contract says otherwise), and MFMA section 65(2)(e) puts the same 30 days on municipalities. Reality check from government's own reporting: at the end of Q3 2025/26, over 90,000 invoices worth R15.5 billion sat unpaid beyond 30 days, overwhelmingly in provinces. Protect yourself: invoice exactly as the order requires, get a received stamp or acknowledgement, diarise day 30, and escalate in writing to the department's CFO and, failing that, the relevant treasury. Price the risk of slow payment into your cash-flow planning rather than discovering it.

## Step 7 — Verify every unsolicited order (the Government Order Scam)
Treasury's CSD campaign describes the pattern: a fake RFQ on convincing stationery, a quote you "win" almost immediately, then delivery to a fake site — or a specification only one (fictitious) supplier can meet, to whom you pay a deposit that vanishes. Defences, from Treasury and GCIS: never use the contact details printed on the suspicious document — find the department's supply-chain unit independently and confirm; check the opportunity exists on the eTenders portal or Tender Bulletin; treat any request to pay a deposit to a named third-party supplier as the scam it is; and GCIS offers 012 473 0000 to verify purchase orders claiming to come from government. No legitimate buyer makes you pay to receive an order.

## What to expect and how to measure it
RFQ wins usually come before tender wins: the CSD + commodities setup starts producing invitations without any bid writing. Track: CSD status (active, addresses current), TCS status, RFQs received per month, quotes submitted vs won, bids submitted vs disqualified (and the stated reason — disqualification reasons are your to-do list), and days-to-payment per client. Expect the first tender to take a full working week to prepare properly.

## Common mistakes and when to get help
Letting the CSD profile lapse on the proof-of-address rule; a non-compliant tax status at submission time; retyped or unsigned forms; claiming preference points the old B-BBEE-level way; missing a compulsory briefing named in the tender; late delivery of the bid box by minutes; and spending money on an unverified "order". For construction work, CIDB registration and grading is its own gate — handle it before bidding. Industry bodies and SEDFA's business-development support can review your first bid pack; a specialist consultant only makes sense for large tenders, and never one who claims to "secure" awards.
