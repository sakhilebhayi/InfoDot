Employing your first person triggers a precise set of legal duties — written particulars, minimum wage, three registrations, monthly payroll filings — and almost all of it is cheaper than a single CCMA dispute. This entry is the checklist in the order the deadlines bite, with the small-employer concessions the law actually gives (and the one it doesn't). Rand amounts change on a schedule: the minimum wage resets every March, so verify current figures on labour.gov.za and sars.gov.za before relying on them.

## The problem
First-time employers trip on four things. Pay built on folklore: the national minimum wage is R30,23 per ordinary hour from 1 March 2026 (Government Gazette 54075, and the same rate now applies to farm and domestic workers) — and it EXCLUDES allowances, payments in kind, tips and bonuses, so "R25 an hour plus tips" is below minimum. Paper skipped: the BCEA requires written particulars of employment from day one, and the belief that tiny employers are exempt is a half-truth — with fewer than five employees you are excused only items (n)-(p) of the list, record-keeping (s31) and payslip detail (s33), never the core written particulars (s28–29). Registrations missed: COIDA gives you SEVEN DAYS from your first employee's start to register with the Compensation Fund; SARS gives 21 business days for PAYE. And dismissal done casually: since 4 September 2025 a new Code of Good Practice on Dismissal applies — it is friendlier to small business on formality, but a probation dismissal with no guidance given and no chance to respond is still the classic lost CCMA case.

## Who this is for
Any sole proprietor, company or CC in South Africa hiring employee number one — or realising, some employees in, that the paperwork never happened.

## Step 1 — Issue written particulars on or before day one
BCEA section 29 lists what must be in writing when employment starts: employer and employee details, job description, place of work, start date, hours and days, the wage or rate and how it's calculated, overtime rate, other payments and payments in kind, pay frequency, deductions, leave, and notice period. A letter of appointment covers it — the law demands written particulars, not a notarised contract. Explain it in a language the employee understands, reissue in writing when anything changes, and keep it for 3 years after they leave.

## Step 2 — Pay at or above the floor, calculated correctly
R30,23 per ordinary hour (from 1 March 2026; reset annually — re-verify each February on labour.gov.za). Count only the wage: allowances, tips, bonuses and payments in kind don't count toward the minimum. Check whether a sectoral determination or bargaining council sets a HIGHER floor for your industry before setting pay.

## Step 3 — Set hours and overtime inside the BCEA
Ordinary hours: at most 45 a week (max 9 a day on a 5-day week, 8 on a 6-day week). Overtime only by agreement, capped at 10 hours a week, paid at 1.5× — or by agreement, paid time off instead. Leave: 21 consecutive days' annual leave per 12-month cycle (or 1 day per 17 worked); sick leave on a 36-month cycle equal to six weeks' normal working days, accruing at 1 day per 26 worked in the first six months. Notice of termination must be written: 1 week (≤6 months' service), 2 weeks (6–12 months), 4 weeks (1 year+). (Employees earning above the BCEA earnings threshold — set by gazette, around R269,600/year from 1 May 2026, verify the exact figure in GN 7384 — fall outside the hours/overtime protections.)

## Step 4 — Register with SARS: PAYE, UIF, SDL in one application
Within 21 business days of becoming an employer, register via eFiling or the EMP101e (one application covers PAYE, SDL and UIF). SDL (1% of payroll) only applies once you expect total salaries above R500,000 over the next 12 months — most first-time employers don't. Then monthly, within 7 days after month-end: the EMP201 declaration and payment of PAYE plus UIF (1% employee + 1% employer, on pay up to R17,712/month). Twice a year, the EMP501 reconciliation produces the IRP5/IT3(a) certificates.

## Step 5 — Register with the Compensation Fund within 7 days
COIDA registration (form W.As.2 or online) is due within 7 days of employing your first employee — the tightest deadline in the set. Then file the annual Return of Earnings when the Fund opens its window each year and pay the assessment: those three things keep you in "good standing", and the Letter of Good Standing they unlock is routinely demanded by tenders and corporate clients. The Fund covers your employees for work injuries and diseases — it's protection, not just compliance.

## Step 6 — Confirm the UIF channel before you file
UIF registration normally rides on the SARS registration; employers not registered with SARS for employees' tax register with the UI Commissioner directly. One moving part: the UIF's online channels have been migrating (the employer uFiling functions were scheduled to move to a new UIF Online platform during 2026) — phone the UIF (0800 030 007) or check labour.gov.za for the current employer portal rather than trusting an old bookmark.

## Step 7 — Run probation the way the 2025 Code requires
The Code of Good Practice: Dismissal (in force since 4 September 2025, replacing the old Schedule 8 code) treats probation as a genuine evaluation period: set its length in advance and keep it reasonable; give real guidance — instruction, training, counselling — scaled to the size of your operation; and before dismissing or extending, give the employee the chance to make representations and consider them. The Code accepts LESS formality from small employers and says reasons for dismissal during probation "may be less compelling" — but probation may never be used to deny permanence (dismissing at probation's end and hiring a replacement is the named abuse). Keep dated notes of the guidance you gave; they are the whole case later.

## Step 8 — Know the dispute clock
An unfair-dismissal dispute goes to the CCMA on form LRA 7.11 within 30 days of dismissal; conciliation is free to the parties. For you as the employer this cuts both ways: follow Step 7's process and keep records, and if a referral arrives, respond — a no-show loses by default. The CCMA/BUSA "SME Labour Support" site (smelaboursupport.org.za) publishes free small-employer templates and guides for exactly this territory.

## What to expect and how to measure it
Compliance is checkable in an afternoon: written particulars signed and filed for every employee; wage ≥ the current minimum, correctly computed; SARS employer registration done and EMP201s filed by the 7th each month; COIDA registered, Return of Earnings current, Letter of Good Standing in hand; a probation file with dated guidance notes. The costs are small and fixed; the failure modes (back-pay orders, penalties, a CCMA award) are not.

## Common mistakes and when to get help
Counting tips or allowances toward the minimum wage; believing "under five staff = no paperwork" (only parts fall away); missing the 7-day COIDA window; registering PAYE "when the business is bigger" (the duty starts with employee one); verbal-only employment terms; probation dismissals with no guidance given and no hearing; and using pre-September-2025 disciplinary templates that cite the repealed Schedule 8. Free help first: the Department of Employment and Labour (012 309 4000; inspections 0860 101 018) answers BCEA questions, and SME Labour Support has the templates. Pay a labour consultant or attorney when a dismissal, retrenchment or CCMA referral is actually on the table — before acting, not after.
