Most small businesses buy software backwards: they watch a demo, get excited, subscribe, and only then discover the feature they actually needed is on a higher tier, their accountant cannot work with it, and leaving means losing their data. This entry is the selection method that prevents that — requirements first, total cost second, a real trial third, exit terms always — with South African pricing examples checked on the vendors' own pages. Prices change and promotions mislead, so treat every rand figure here as "as of 31 October 2026" and reconfirm on the vendor's page before deciding.

## The problem
Four failure patterns recur in the credible guidance. Buying complexity you do not need — "complexity also adds more cost… especially if you must hire a consultant" (SBA's software-selection checklist). Choosing without written requirements, so demos decide instead of needs (NI Business Info's method exists precisely to prevent this). Ignoring the costs beyond the subscription — add-ons, extra users, training, migration, and the price after the promotion ends (GOV.UK frames this as understanding total cost of ownership). And lock-in: contracts and data traps that make switching "difficult, time consuming and disproportionately expensive" (GOV.UK's lock-in guidance) — a risk with a South African twist, because the Consumer Protection Act's fixed-term cancellation rights in section 14 explicitly do not apply to transactions between juristic persons, so a company generally cannot lean on the CPA to escape a bad software contract. Read the contract.

## Who this is for
Any small business choosing its first accounting, CRM, inventory or payroll system, or replacing one — without paying a consultant to choose for it.

## Step 1 — Write the five jobs before you see any demo
List the specific jobs the software must do for you ("send quotes and invoices with my VAT number", "show who owes me money", "run payslips for 4 staff with UIF"). Then grade every requirement must-have / advantageous / nice-to-have, and discard any package that fails a must-have — that is NI Business Info's whole method, and it works because it makes the shortlist mechanical instead of emotional.

## Step 2 — Add the non-negotiables for a South African business
- **Statutory fit**: a payroll tool must handle PAYE, UIF and SDL and produce what SARS's employer cycle needs — monthly EMP201 declarations, twice-yearly EMP501 reconciliations, IRP5/IT3(a) certificates (SARS employer-declaration requirements). An accounting tool must handle 15% VAT invoicing if you are a vendor.
- **Your accountant**: can they work in it or exchange data with it? (NI Business Info puts this on the checklist for good reason — fighting your accountant's workflow costs you fees.)
- **POPIA**: a vendor holding your customer or staff data is an "operator" — you need a written contract in which they commit to security safeguards, and they must notify you immediately of unauthorised access (POPIA section 21). If they host abroad, section 72 requires the recipient to be bound by adequate protection. Established vendors publish a data-processing agreement; its absence is a red flag.

## Step 3 — Shortlist two or three and cost them fully
Prefer vendors with testimonials from businesses your size and the financial strength to keep updating the product (SBA). Then cost beyond the headline: add-ons and extra users, migration of your data, training time, and — critically — the post-promotion price. The gap is not small: as of 31 October 2026, Xero South Africa's entry plan lists at R450/month but new-customer promotions show R90 for the first three months (and Xero's own notice raises the regular price to R485 from 1 November 2026); QuickBooks Online ZA lists Simple Start at R322/month with a 90%-off-for-6-months promotion showing R32.20 — and taking the promotion forfeits the free trial. Also compare like with like on VAT: Xero and Sage show VAT-inclusive prices, while Zoho and others price before VAT.

## Step 4 — Know the genuinely free tiers from the trials
Verified ongoing free tiers (as of 31 October 2026, per each vendor's page): Zoho Books' free plan (one user plus an accountant, capped — Zoho's FAQ ties it to revenue under R1 million a year), Zoho CRM free for 3 users, HubSpot's free CRM for 2 users, Zoho Inventory free for 50 orders a month. Everything else in the mainstream set is a time-limited trial (14–30 days). Two currency notes: HubSpot and Pipedrive bill in US dollars, which adds exchange-rate exposure; and a free tier's terms are the vendor's to change. For scale, the common SA-priced paid options checked: Sage Accounting Start R270/month incl. VAT; Zoho Books Standard R99/month ex VAT; Sage Business Cloud Payroll from R97/month incl. VAT for the smallest band; SimplePay prices per employee by quote.

## Step 5 — Trial with your own real records
A free trial is for a hands-on evaluation, a week or two (SBA). Load your actual customers, items and a month of real transactions, and run each of your five jobs end to end — including the awkward ones (a credit note, a VAT report, a payslip with overtime). Test the integrations you will rely on (bank feeds, your accountant's access) rather than trusting the logo wall — and avoid building custom integrations, which the SBA checklist singles out as a cost trap.

## Step 6 — Check the exit before you enter
Before paying: Can you export all your data, in a usable format, yourself? (GOV.UK: retain ownership and access to any data held by third parties.) Is the contract month-to-month or cancel-anytime (SBA's low-risk-terms advice)? Where is the data hosted, and is the POPIA operator contract in place? GOV.UK's lock-in guidance is balanced — some lock-in can be worth accepting for value — but accept it knowingly, never by default, and be wary of an obscure tool chosen for one feature that doesn't use common standards.

## Step 7 — Start low, upgrade late
Subscribe to the lowest tier that does your five jobs, on monthly billing, and upgrade when a real limit bites. Development-bank guidance (BDC, Canada) notes most small businesses start off-the-shelf and outgrow it in a few years — so the vendor's upgrade path matters more than buying the big tier today.

## Common mistakes and when to get help
Choosing from demos instead of written requirements; comparing a promotional price with a regular one, or a VAT-inclusive price with an exclusive one, or annual-billed with monthly-billed rates; ignoring statutory needs until filing season; skipping the accountant-compatibility check; signing long contracts for untested software; never testing the data export; and building custom integrations. Get professional help for the bookkeeping setup (chart of accounts, VAT configuration) rather than the software choice — an hour of your accountant's time configuring the tool you chose with this method is money well spent.
