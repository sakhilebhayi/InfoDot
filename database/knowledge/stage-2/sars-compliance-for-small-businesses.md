Tax compliance for a small South African business is a handful of registrations, a calendar of deadlines, and one genuinely valuable choice: which tax regime fits your size. Getting it right is mostly administrative; getting it wrong compounds monthly, because SARS's administrative penalties recur every month until you fix the gap. This entry maps the registrations, the 2026 thresholds (which changed — most online guides are outdated), the regimes, the deadlines, and the penalty mechanics. Thresholds and dates below were read from SARS pages on 31 October 2026 and can change; the SARS website is the only safe reference.

## The problem
Three traps catch small businesses. Outdated thresholds: the VAT registration threshold moved on 1 April 2026 — SARS now applies a compulsory threshold of R2.3 million (up from R1 million) and a voluntary threshold of R120,000, while noting the amending legislation was still awaiting promulgation (SARS states it is applying the new levels administratively to new registrations; at least one older SARS page still shows R1 million, so even SARS's own pages disagree — check the VAT "Register" page, not cached guides). Missed registration windows: most SARS registrations carry a 21-business-day clock. And regime blindness: many micro and small companies pay standard 27% company tax when turnover tax or small-business-corporation rates would legally tax them far less.

## Who this is for
Anyone starting or running a sole proprietorship, company or close corporation in South Africa — especially in the first two years, when the registrations and the regime choice land.

## Step 1 — Get registered, the right way around
A company registers with CIPC first; SARS then generates its income-tax reference number automatically, and you activate it on eFiling (secure.sarsefiling.co.za). A sole proprietor or partner registers directly with SARS within 21 business days of becoming liable. Keep your registered details current — address changes must reach SARS within 21 business days.

## Step 2 — Choose your regime deliberately
- **Turnover tax (micro businesses)**: elective, for annual turnover up to R2.3 million (raised from R1m, effective 1 April 2026), not available to personal-service providers or labour brokers. It replaces income tax, provisional tax, CGT and dividends tax with a tax on turnover at startlingly low rates: 0% on the first R600,000 of turnover, 1% of the amount above R600,000 up to R950,000, then R3,500 + 2% up to R1.4m, then R12,500 + 3% above that — a maximum of R39,500 of tax at R2.3m of turnover. Register through the SARS Online Query System; pay twice a year (end August and end February) with a final TT03 return.
- **Small business corporation (SBC) rates**: a company qualifying under section 12E (all shareholders natural persons, gross income ≤ R20m, no more than 20% of receipts from investment income and personal services, not a personal service provider) pays graduated rates instead of flat 27% — for years ending 1 April 2026–31 March 2027: 0% up to R99,000 of taxable income, 7% to R365,000, 21% to R550,000, 27% above.
- **Standard company tax**: 27%, plus the full provisional-tax cycle.
A business that qualifies for turnover tax or SBC rates and doesn't use them is donating money; a session with an accountant to confirm eligibility typically pays for itself immediately.

## Step 3 — Watch the VAT threshold as a rolling number
Compulsory VAT registration is triggered when taxable supplies in ANY 12-month period exceed (or will likely exceed) R2.3 million — and you must apply within 21 business days. Voluntary registration opens at R120,000, which can make sense when your customers are VAT vendors who want tax invoices. Caveat to respect: SARS describes the new thresholds as applied administratively from 1 April 2026 with the law's promulgation pending and existing registrations not cancelled automatically — so if you're near either line, read SARS's current VAT pages before acting. Registered vendors file (mostly) two-monthly VAT returns, due by the 25th or the last business day of the month on eFiling, and keep tax invoices and records for 5 years.

## Step 4 — The provisional-tax calendar
Companies are automatically provisional taxpayers; individuals with business, rental or interest income are too. For a February year-end the rhythm is: first payment by the end of August, second by the last business day of February, optional third top-up after year-end — all via IRP6 on eFiling — and the annual return (ITR14 for companies) within 12 months of year-end. Underestimating taxable income on the second payment attracts penalties and interest, so estimate honestly and update at the second payment.

## Step 5 — The moment you employ, three more registrations
Register as an employer within 21 business days of becoming one (the trigger: any employee liable for normal tax). Then monthly: the EMP201 declaration and payment of PAYE + UIF by the 7th of the following month (or the preceding business day). UIF is 1% from the employee plus 1% from you, on pay up to R17,712/month. SDL (1% of payroll) only applies once expected annual payroll exceeds R500,000. Twice a year, the EMP501 reconciliation ties it all together and produces employees' IRP5/IT3(a) certificates — late EMP501s cost 1% of annual PAYE per month, up to 10%.

## Step 6 — Keep the records SARS actually demands
Five years from the date of submitting each return, longer if an audit is open: invoices, accounting records, payroll records; for turnover-tax businesses, records of amounts received, dividends declared, and assets/liabilities over R10,000. Digital copies on a backed-up system satisfy this and survive fires.

## Step 7 — Use eFiling for everything, and trust no "SARS" message asking for credentials
eFiling (and the SARS MobiApp) files returns, shows outstanding returns and debt, and issues the Tax Compliance Status that funders and tenders require. SARS states plainly that it never asks for passwords, OTPs or eFiling credentials — any message that does is phishing.

## What to expect and how to measure it
Compliance is measured by SARS itself: your Tax Compliance Status on eFiling. Keep it green and screenshot-ready — funders (SEDFA, NEF, banks) and every government tender ask for it. The cost of slipping is concrete: administrative penalties for outstanding returns run R250 to R16,000 PER MONTH, recurring until you comply (remission can be requested only after the returns are in); understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act reach 200%; failing to submit returns is a criminal offence; and directors who run a company's finances negligently or fraudulently can be personally liable for its tax debts.

## Common mistakes and when to get help
Planning around pre-2026 thresholds; missing a 21-business-day registration window; filing the EMP201 late "just this month"; underestimating the second provisional payment; registering for VAT voluntarily without the admin capacity to file properly; losing five-year records in a laptop theft; and answering phishing. Get an accountant or registered tax practitioner for the regime choice (turnover tax vs SBC vs standard — the savings dwarf the fee), for VAT registration mechanics near the threshold, and the first EMP501 reconciliation. SARS's own help: 0800 00 7277, the eBooking service, and the Tax Guide for Small Businesses.
