Registering a business in South Africa is not one registration — it is a short chain of them across four bodies: CIPC, SARS, the Department of Employment and Labour and, for some trades, your municipality. Each is simple on its own; businesses get into trouble because nothing forces the later ones until a penalty arrives. This entry lists the chain in the order the dependencies actually run, with the official portals. Every figure carries an "as at" date because these numbers change.

## The problem
Owners register a company, open the doors, and assume the paperwork is done. The verified consequences of stopping there: CIPC refers a company for deregistration when its annual returns are two successive years in arrears — a deregistered company ceases to exist and CIPC's guidance warns that its directors may be held liable for debts. CIPC also blocks annual-return filing until beneficial ownership has been filed. Employers who register with SARS but skip the Department of Employment and Labour discover it at claim time, and a late Compensation Fund return attracts interest and penalties. Trading in a licensed trade (prepared food, health services, certain entertainment) without a municipal licence is an offence under the Businesses Act.

## Who this is for
A sole proprietor does not register with CIPC at all — SARS taxes the profits in your personal capacity — but the SARS, employer, licence and POPIA duties below still apply to you. A private company (Pty Ltd) follows the whole chain. Any employer, whatever the structure, carries the employer registrations.

## Step 1 — Register the company with CIPC
Register on BizPortal (bizportal.gov.za, run by CIPC) or CIPC e-Services. As at October 2026 a company registration costs R125 plus R50 for a name reservation (R175 with a name). BizPortal can also start the business bank account with one of the big four banks during registration.

## Step 2 — File beneficial ownership within 10 business days
File the company's beneficial-ownership declaration with CIPC within 10 business days of incorporation (anyone holding more than 5% is declarable), and update it within 10 business days of any change. CIPC will not accept your annual return until this is on file.

## Step 3 — Open the business bank account
With the CIPC documents, if you did not do it through BizPortal. Route every business receipt and payment through it from day one — the bookkeeping entry on InfoDot covers why.

## Step 4 — Set up with SARS
A new company is registered for income tax automatically through SARS's direct interface with CIPC; your job is to set up the eFiling profile and run provisional tax (IRP6 instalments). A sole proprietor declares business profits in their personal return and usually becomes a provisional taxpayer.

VAT is separate and turnover-driven: registration is compulsory once taxable supplies in any consecutive 12 months exceed — or will exceed — R2.3 million (the threshold rose from R1 million on 1 April 2026; as at October 2026 on sars.gov.za). Apply within 21 business days of becoming liable, on eFiling. Voluntary registration is possible from R120,000 of taxable supplies in the past 12 months, in defined circumstances. Watch your rolling 12-month turnover so the threshold never surprises you.

## Step 5 — The day you hire your first employee
Two sides, both compulsory. SARS side: register as an employer within 21 business days (eFiling, or form EMP101e). The PAYE registration includes UIF collection; SDL is 1% of payroll but you are exempt while expected salaries for the next 12 months stay under R500,000. UIF contributions are 1% from you plus 1% from the employee, on pay up to a ceiling of R17,712 per month (figures as at October 2026). The monthly EMP201 return and payment are due within seven days after month-end.

Department of Employment and Labour side: register with the UIF itself (forms UI-8 and UI-19; the department's older pages and a newer notice differ on the current channel, so confirm whether BizPortal or uFiling applies when you register), and register with the Compensation Fund within 7 days of appointing employees. Each year after that, submit the Return of Earnings on ROE Online (cfonline.labour.gov.za), pay the assessment, and keep the Letter of Good Standing current — many customers and sites will ask for it.

## Step 6 — Municipal licence for licensed trades
If your trade is in Schedule 1 of the Businesses Act 71 of 1991 — selling or preparing meals and perishable food, certain health and entertainment businesses — apply for a trading licence at your municipality before trading. Processes and fees differ by municipality, so confirm locally. Food premises also need a Certificate of Acceptability from municipal environmental health.

## Step 7 — POPIA: register your Information Officer
Under POPIA the head of the business is its Information Officer by default. Register the Information Officer on the Information Regulator's eServices portal (eservices.inforegulator.org.za) — it is free, and the Regulator encourages every business to register. POPIA's wider duties (lawful processing, security safeguards, breach notification) apply whether or not you have registered.

## Step 8 — The annual cycle
Every year: file the CIPC annual return within 30 business days of the incorporation anniversary (close corporations: from the anniversary month to the end of the following month), with beneficial ownership confirmed first, the year's turnover declared, and financial statements or the Financial Accountability Supplement uploaded. Keep the SARS filing calendar (provisional tax, the annual return, EMP201s and EMP501 reconciliations if you employ) and the Compensation Fund Return of Earnings. Two missed CIPC annual returns in a row start deregistration.

## Tools and resources
BizPortal (bizportal.gov.za) for company registration and linked services; CIPC e-Services and annualreturns.cipc.co.za; SARS eFiling and the SARS small-business pages (sars.gov.za, Businesses and Employers section); ROE Online at cfonline.labour.gov.za; the Information Regulator's eServices portal. Free business support: SEDA's advice and training services (now part of SEDFA, the merged small-enterprise agency — confirm current programmes at source).

## How you know you are compliant
A short checklist you can produce on demand: this year's CIPC annual-return and beneficial-ownership confirmations; a green tax-compliance status on eFiling; EMP201s filed and paid by the 7th each month; a current Compensation Fund Letter of Good Standing; the municipal licence displayed if your trade needs one; and your Information Officer registration reference.

## Common mistakes and when to get help
The classic mistakes: assuming the CIPC certificate finishes the job; missing the beneficial-ownership filing and only finding out when the annual return bounces; skipping the Department of Employment and Labour because SARS already collects UIF money (the registrations are separate); letting two annual returns lapse; and working off the old R1 million VAT threshold.

Use a registered professional for VAT or payroll disputes, provisional-tax planning, customising a company's MOI, and licensing appeals. Anyone you pay for tax services must themselves be registered with a Recognised Controlling Body and with SARS as a tax practitioner — check before you hire.
