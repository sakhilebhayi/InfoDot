# R-67: homepage and authentication messaging (owner brief, 2026-11-26, verbatim)

INFODOT — HOMEPAGE AND AUTHENTICATION MESSAGING TRANSFORMATION
Let's continue refining InfoDot's brand messaging, starting with the homepage and then moving on to the authentication pages (login, registration, password recovery and any related entry points).
This is not simply a copywriting exercise. I want us to improve how InfoDot communicates its value, connects emotionally with business owners and convinces visitors that joining the ecosystem can make a meaningful difference to how they start, manage and grow their businesses.
The messaging must be human, emotionally engaging, confident, relatable and persuasive. Every section should contribute to one coherent story.
1. Start with the homepage hero message
Keep the approved hero messaging:
Heading:
A better, more connected way to build business.
Supporting message:
We connect you to the knowledge, tools and business opportunities you need to start, manage and grow.
This establishes InfoDot's overall promise. The sections that follow must reinforce and expand on this promise rather than repeat it.
2. Transform the section directly below the hero
The current heading:
"Most small businesses run on memory and chat"
needs to be replaced with a much more emotionally engaging and thought-provoking message.
I want to explore a stronger direction:
"The truth is, most businesses don't really know what they're doing. InfoDot is here to change that."
The intention is to introduce an uncomfortable but relatable business reality: many people start businesses with ambition, but are left figuring everything out as they go.
They may be:

* Making important decisions without knowing where to find reliable business guidance.
* Managing operations through scattered spreadsheets, WhatsApp conversations, emails and disconnected tools.
* Repeating mistakes because they lack access to practical knowledge and proven solutions.
* Struggling to find the right tools to organise and manage their businesses.
* Missing opportunities because they don't know where to find the right people, businesses, suppliers or connections.
* Trying to grow without a clear understanding of what their next steps should be.

The message must make visitors pause and think:
"That's exactly what running my business feels like."
However, be careful with the tone. I want bold, honest and slightly provocative messaging, not messaging that insults or humiliates business owners. The objective is to challenge the way business is currently being done and position InfoDot as a better way forward.
You may refine the proposed heading if you can produce something stronger, but preserve its intended meaning and emotional impact.
3. Rewrite the supporting messages around the new heading
The supporting messages must directly explain the problem introduced by the heading and demonstrate how InfoDot addresses it.
Do not use generic statements such as "we help businesses succeed" or simply list platform features.
Build the narrative around the actual gaps InfoDot is designed to fill.
Explore messaging that communicates the following:
Knowledge — Stop figuring everything out alone.
Businesses need access to information, guidance, practical solutions and the experience of others. Show how InfoDot helps users find knowledge and answers that can help them make better-informed business decisions.
Tools — Stop running everything through disconnected systems.
Businesses need practical tools to organise, manage and operate their activities. Explain how the interconnected Dot platforms help bring important business activities together.
Connections — Stop missing opportunities because you don't know where to look.
Businesses need to discover other businesses, services, resources and opportunities. Explain how InfoDot helps bring businesses and opportunities closer together.
Progress — Turn what you learn into action.
InfoDot should communicate that it is more than a place to read information or browse software. It gives businesses access to resources and capabilities they can actually use to take practical steps forward.
These are messaging directions, not fixed headings. Develop compelling copy that communicates each value proposition naturally.
4. Build an emotional connection with the visitor
I want the homepage to speak to the reality of running a business.
Make visitors recognise the frustration of:

* Not knowing what to do next.
* Having to figure everything out alone.
* Feeling overwhelmed by the different responsibilities of running a business.
* Spending too much time trying to find information and suitable tools.
* Having ambitions for their business but lacking the resources or connections to pursue them effectively.

Then introduce a different possibility.
Imagine having access to business knowledge, practical tools, solutions and connections within one ecosystem. Imagine not having to search across countless disconnected platforms to find resources that can help you move forward.
This is the emotional opportunity InfoDot should communicate.
Make visitors feel:

* Understood: InfoDot recognises the realities of running a business.
* Relieved: There is a more organised and connected way to approach business.
* Empowered: They can access resources that help them make progress.
* Confident: They don't have to figure everything out alone.
* Inspired: Their business has room to develop and grow.

Do not promise guaranteed business success, instant growth or outcomes that InfoDot cannot substantiate. Position InfoDot as an ecosystem that equips businesses with better access to the resources, knowledge, tools and connections that can support their progress.
5. Make the value of joining InfoDot unmistakable
The visitor must understand why they should register.
Do not leave them with the impression that InfoDot is just another website with a collection of business applications.
Communicate that joining InfoDot gives businesses access to an interconnected ecosystem designed to support their business journey.
The messaging should make the following clear:

* They can access business knowledge and practical guidance.
* They can discover and use tools designed to support different business activities.
* They can connect with other businesses and explore opportunities.
* They can build their business presence and manage their team.
* They can access an ecosystem that supports different stages of business development.
* They can start exploring the ecosystem without an upfront registration fee, provided this remains consistent with the actual current registration and platform-access model.

Make the benefits tangible. Visitors should be able to imagine how InfoDot could fit into their everyday business activities.
6. Continue the messaging through the rest of the homepage
Review the entire homepage and ensure that every section contributes to the same narrative.
The flow should feel intentional:

1. The promise: A better, more connected way to build business.
2. The reality: Many businesses are trying to operate without the knowledge, tools and connections they need.
3. The gap: Disconnected information, scattered tools, limited guidance and missed opportunities make business harder than it needs to be.
4. The solution: InfoDot brings knowledge, tools, businesses and opportunities together.
5. The possibilities: Show how the Dot ecosystem helps businesses start, manage, connect and grow.
6. The transformation: Help visitors see how being part of a connected business ecosystem could improve the way they approach their business.
7. The invitation: Make joining InfoDot feel like a natural and worthwhile next step.

Avoid repeating the same message in every section. Each section should introduce a new reason to understand, trust or explore InfoDot.
7. Extend the same messaging into the authentication pages
Once the homepage messaging is refined, continue the same brand narrative into the authentication experience.
This includes:

* Login page.
* Registration page.
* Password recovery page.
* Account verification screens.
* Any other authentication-related entry points.

The authentication pages should not feel disconnected from the homepage.
For example, when someone clicks Register after discovering InfoDot, the registration experience should reinforce the feeling that they are taking the first step towards a better-equipped business.
The registration page should communicate the opportunity awaiting them, without overwhelming them with marketing text or distracting them from completing registration.
The login page should feel familiar, confident and welcoming.
Password recovery and verification pages should remain clear, reassuring and helpful.
Preserve the existing centralised InfoDot authentication architecture and all current authentication functionality. Do not introduce separate authentication systems for individual Dot platforms.
8. Design and presentation requirements
The messaging must be supported by the visual experience.

* Maintain InfoDot's established brand identity.
* Use strong typographic hierarchy to make important messages stand out.
* Ensure headings, supporting copy and calls to action work together.
* Avoid overcrowding sections with excessive text.
* Use visual storytelling where it genuinely strengthens the message.
* Maintain the premium, modern, professional and welcoming design direction.
* Use subtle animations and scroll-based transitions where appropriate.
* Ensure all sections work beautifully on desktop, tablet and mobile.
* Maintain accessibility, readability and performance.

The words and design must reinforce one another. Do not simply insert new copy into the existing layout if the layout needs to change to communicate the message effectively.
9. Research and implementation requirements
Before making changes:

1. Inspect the current homepage and all authentication pages.
2. Review the existing InfoDot platform directory and actual available functionality.
3. Understand the current registration, login and onboarding journeys.
4. Identify existing messaging that is repetitive, weak, unclear or disconnected.
5. Map the proposed messaging to actual InfoDot capabilities.

Do not invent features, business success statistics, testimonials, user numbers or platform capabilities.
Do not remove working functionality or break existing navigation, registration, authentication, SEO, analytics or responsive behaviour.
Implement the improvements in the existing architecture using the established Laravel, Livewire and Tailwind conventions.
After implementation, test the complete visitor journey from landing on the homepage through to registration and login. Inspect the actual rendered pages on desktop and mobile.
10. Final objective
I want someone visiting InfoDot for the first time to experience three things:
First: Recognition. They recognise the challenges InfoDot is talking about and feel that the platform understands the reality of running a business.
Second: Possibility. They understand that there is a better, more connected way to access business knowledge, tools and opportunities.
Third: Motivation. They see a compelling reason to join InfoDot and begin using the ecosystem to support their business journey.
The messaging must make one thing unmistakably clear:
InfoDot is not just another platform asking businesses to sign up. It is an interconnected business ecosystem designed to help them become better equipped to start, manage, connect and grow.
Make the problem relatable. Make the solution tangible. Make the value personal. Make joining feel worthwhile.
The ultimate test: A business owner should finish reading the homepage and understand not only what InfoDot is, but why having InfoDot in their corner could make a meaningful difference to the way they build and run their business.
