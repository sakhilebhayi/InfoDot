# POPIA readiness (draft, 2026-10-27)

**This is an engineering readiness draft, not legal advice and not a compliance claim.** It records what the source code of the 25 Laravel repositories shows, what it does not show, and the decisions that only the responsible party (and whoever advises it on POPIA) can make. Nothing here has been reviewed by a lawyer or by an Information Officer. Statements are marked **verified** (with where), **inferred** (from column or file names) or **unknown**.

Input: `POPIA_INVENTORY.md`, generated by `tools/popia-inventory.py` from `origin/main` of each repository on 2026-10-27 (regenerate it before relying on it: `python3 tools/popia-inventory.py --md docs/programme/POPIA_INVENTORY.md <repo folders>`). It reads column **names** in migrations, model casts, dependencies, `.env.example` and views. It cannot see data, free-text content, the production database, the production `.env`, or whether a feature is used.

## 3. Security compromise notification (s22) and the access-log result

If personal information may have been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, the responsible party must notify the Information Regulator and the data subjects as soon as reasonably possible after discovery. The access-log review (2026-10-27) found no sign of a third party on the platform hosts in the exposure windows (every answered request matches our own probes by time and user agent; not proven by address). It found **7 unexplained answered requests on two other sites** (doc.infodot.co.za, 2026-10-01, 16 bytes each; infodot.co.za, `.env`-type 868 bytes on 2026-10-05 and `.git`-type 281 bytes on 2026-10-08). **The s22 question for InfoDot cannot be answered until the owner runs the private check in `ACCESS_LOG_REVIEW.md`.** If the 868-byte file was a real `.env`, treat the InfoDot secrets as copied, rotate, and take adviser input on notification. The data the exposed databases held on 2026-10-20 (Dopemine, Engage, Memory, Sheet) and 2026-10-23 (Charts) was fetched by our own check; the copies, if any were kept, are on the machine that ran it.

## 4. Open decisions for the owner (none is blocked on engineering)

1. Which legal entity is the responsible party for each platform and for Mines/Charts, and who is the Information Officer; register with the Information Regulator.
2. Hosting location of the cPanel host and of every processor (host, Paystack, Stripe, Sentry, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Fonts) and whether each transfer meets s72 (agreement, adequate protection, or consent).
3. Retention periods per data class (accounts, leave records, audit logs, GPS, session IPs, backups) and who signs them off.
4. Whether Mines operator tracking (GPS, emergency contacts, photos) has a documented purpose and notice to the operators.
5. What each Anthropic/OpenAI call sends; remove personal data from prompts, or accept and document it.
6. Self-host fonts and scripts (a design and engineering change that removes the third-party IP exposure for Google Fonts and unpkg), or accept and disclose.
7. Finish the access-log decision for infodot.co.za and doc.infodot.co.za.
8. A PAIA manual, one ecosystem privacy policy text (and the BluePin/BluPin spelling), and a breach response plan with the s22 steps.

## 5. What engineering can do next (once the decisions above are made)

- Subject-request runbook and a read-only "find this person" script across the 25 databases (read-only, counts first).
- Prune and purge jobs tied to the retention schedule.
- Encrypt contact and identity columns beyond HR where the decision is yes (needs the `secrets:reencrypt` pattern and a plan for blind indexes on searchable columns).
- Self-host Inter and the other fonts and scripts; remove the unused Pusher and AI configuration where it is dead.
- Re-run `tools/popia-inventory.py` after each of these and attach the diff to the pull request.
