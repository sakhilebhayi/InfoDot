InfoDot – Verified Business Solutions Research & Knowledge Seeding
1. Objective
Build a comprehensive, high-quality library of practical business solutions for InfoDot by researching the internet for business challenges, proven strategies, methods, frameworks, and real-world solutions that have helped businesses succeed.
InfoDot is designed to become a collective mentor for entrepreneurs, startups, small businesses, growing companies, and established business owners.
Since InfoDot is still building its initial user base, we need to seed the platform with valuable, credible, actionable knowledge before onboarding a significant number of users.
The goal is not to fill InfoDot with generic articles or motivational content. The goal is to give business owners access to specific, implementable solutions to real problems they face when starting, operating, managing, and growing a business.
All seeded content must be published under the official InfoDot account, clearly identified as official platform-curated knowledge.
2. Research methodology
Search the internet extensively for business problems and solutions that have demonstrated practical results.
Prioritise information from credible sources, including:

* Government business development agencies and official business resources.
* Universities and recognised business schools.
* Established business consulting and advisory organisations.
* Industry associations and professional bodies.
* Reputable research institutions and business publications.
* Documented case studies from real businesses.
* Experienced entrepreneurs and verified industry practitioners.
* Established organisations with publicly documented operational practices.
* Credible financial, legal, accounting, HR, and management resources.

Use real-world examples, case studies, research findings, and documented business experiences wherever possible.
Do not treat an idea as proven simply because it appears frequently online.
For every recommended solution, establish:

1. What business problem does it address?
2. Why does the problem occur?
3. What evidence supports the proposed solution?
4. Which businesses or industries can benefit from it?
5. What practical steps should a business owner take?
6. What resources, tools, skills, or budget are required?
7. What results should the business owner realistically expect?
8. How should success be measured?
9. What risks, limitations, or common mistakes should be considered?

Clearly distinguish between:

* Research-backed solutions.
* Documented real-world practices.
* Industry-recognised best practices.
* Expert recommendations.
* Emerging approaches that have promising but limited evidence.

Never fabricate statistics, case studies, testimonials, business results, or references.

4. Required content structure
Every solution published to InfoDot must follow a consistent, detailed structure.
Each entry must contain:
1. Solution title
A clear, searchable title describing the problem or outcome.
2. Business category
The category and relevant subcategory.
3. Problem description
Describe the actual business problem in detail, including how it affects daily operations, finances, customers, employees, or growth.
4. Why this problem happens
Explain the underlying causes rather than simply describing the symptoms.
5. Who this solution is for
Identify the business types, sizes, industries, and business stages that would benefit.
6. Recommended solution
Explain the recommended approach and why it works.
7. Step-by-step implementation guide
Provide specific, sequential instructions that a business owner can follow.
Avoid vague instructions such as "improve marketing", "manage finances better", or "increase productivity".
Instead, explain exactly what to do, how to do it, what tools to use, what information is needed, and what outcome each step should produce.
8. Tools and resources
Recommend relevant tools, templates, software, checklists, calculators, or resources.
Where possible, include free and low-cost alternatives for small businesses.
9. Example or case study
Include a real, verifiable example where available.
Clearly identify whether it is a documented case study, a research example, or an illustrative scenario. Never present an invented scenario as a real business success story.
10. Expected outcomes and KPIs
Explain how the business owner can measure whether the solution is working.
Include relevant metrics, measurement methods, and realistic timeframes where evidence supports them.
11. Common mistakes and risks
Identify what could go wrong, what to avoid, and when the solution may not be appropriate.
12. References and further reading
Provide direct links to the original research, reports, case studies, official guidance, or other credible sources used.
Include publication dates where available.
5. Make the knowledge practical and accessible
InfoDot must serve business owners with different levels of experience.
Content should:

* Use clear, professional, straightforward language.
* Explain technical business terminology.
* Avoid unnecessary academic language.
* Include practical examples.
* Break complicated processes into manageable actions.
* Include realistic recommendations for businesses with limited budgets.
* Distinguish between what a business owner can do independently and what requires a qualified professional.
* Include South African considerations where relevant, while also incorporating globally applicable business knowledge.

For South African businesses, investigate relevant requirements and resources from authoritative sources, including CIPC, SARS, the Department of Employment and Labour, the Information Regulator, and recognised business development organisations.
Do not assume that international legislation or business practices automatically apply in South Africa.
6. Content quality and verification standards
Every entry must meet these requirements:

* Research must be conducted before content is created.
* Important factual claims must be supported by credible sources.
* Sources must be directly relevant to the solution.
* Links must lead to the original source wherever possible.
* Do not copy articles or reproduce copyrighted content.
* Synthesise research into original InfoDot guidance.
* Avoid duplicate or substantially similar solutions.
* Do not create shallow articles simply to increase the content count.
* Do not invent evidence, statistics, citations, or implementation results.
* Flag outdated information and regularly changing regulatory requirements.
* Identify when professional legal, financial, tax, or technical advice is necessary.

Each solution should provide enough detail for a business owner to understand the problem and begin implementing the recommended approach without needing to search elsewhere for basic instructions.
7. InfoDot publishing requirements
All researched solutions must be published under the official InfoDot account.
The official account should act as the initial knowledge contributor while the platform builds its community.
For each published entry:

* Attribute the content to the official InfoDot account.
* Assign the correct category and relevant tags.
* Include a concise summary for discovery.
* Include the full detailed solution.
* Attach supporting references.
* Identify the intended business audience.
* Make the content searchable.
* Ensure it can be discovered through relevant business questions.
* Identify whether the content is research-backed, a documented case study, or an industry best practice.
* Include the last-reviewed date where the platform supports it.

Do not create fake users, fake contributors, fake discussions, fake questions, fake answers, or artificial engagement.
The content must be transparently presented as knowledge curated and published by InfoDot.
8. Build a knowledge library, not just a collection of articles
Organise the content so that business owners can discover solutions based on their actual circumstances.
For example:
A business owner asks: "My business makes sales, but I never seem to have enough money to pay suppliers."
InfoDot should be able to surface:

* Cash flow management.
* Accounts receivable management.
* Supplier payment planning.
* Working capital management.
* Cash flow forecasting.
* Pricing and profit margin analysis.

A business owner asks: "I want to start a construction company but don't know where to begin."
InfoDot should surface:

* Business registration.
* Industry-specific legal requirements.
* Startup budgeting.
* Equipment and supplier planning.
* Insurance.
* Finding customers.
* Pricing projects.
* Cash flow planning.
* Contract management.

Solutions should be interconnected through related topics, business stages, industries, and common challenges.
Where supported by the existing InfoDot architecture, structure the knowledge so that Dot.Brains can connect related concepts and Dot.Memory can use relevant business context to improve future recommendations.
9. Initial seeding strategy
Do not attempt to publish thousands of entries without quality control.
Build the library in deliberate stages:
Stage 1 – Essential business survival knowledge
Focus on the most common problems that can cause businesses to fail:

* Cash flow.
* Finding customers.
* Pricing.
* Financial management.
* Customer retention.
* Business planning.
* Legal compliance.
* Operational management.

Stage 2 – Business development
Cover sales, marketing, HR, productivity, customer experience, and management.
Stage 3 – Business growth
Cover scaling, funding, partnerships, automation, technology, and expansion.
Stage 4 – Industry-specific expertise
Develop detailed solution libraries for different industries.
Stage 5 – Advanced business intelligence
Introduce more advanced topics such as financial forecasting, performance management, business analytics, strategic planning, risk management, and AI adoption.
Prioritise content based on business impact, how frequently a problem occurs, how urgent it is, and how useful the solution is to the intended audience.
10. Technical implementation and safety
Before publishing anything, inspect the existing InfoDot codebase, database schema, content models, categories, publishing workflows, and official account implementation.
Reuse the existing architecture wherever possible.
Do not:

* Create duplicate content systems.
* Bypass existing permissions.
* Publish content under an ordinary user account.
* Modify unrelated platform functionality.
* Insert unverified information into the production database.
* Assume database fields or relationships exist without inspecting them.

Use the existing content creation and publishing mechanisms.
Implement appropriate validation, duplicate detection, source tracking, and error handling.
If a suitable workflow does not exist, document the gap and propose the smallest appropriate implementation rather than introducing an unnecessary parallel system.
11. Final objective
Build InfoDot into a trusted, continuously expanding business knowledge platform that can help someone:

* Start a business.
* Solve operational problems.
* Understand their finances.
* Find and retain customers.
* Manage employees.
* Improve productivity.
* Make better decisions.
* Overcome business challenges.
* Grow and scale their organisation.

The content should make a business owner feel that InfoDot understands the challenges they face and can guide them towards practical solutions.
The quality standard: Every published solution should be useful enough that a real business owner could read it, understand it, take action, and measure whether it helped their business.
Start by auditing the existing InfoDot publishing architecture, then research, curate, validate, and seed the initial knowledge library through the official InfoDot account. Work in manageable batches, preserve source attribution, and report what was successfully published and what still requires review.
