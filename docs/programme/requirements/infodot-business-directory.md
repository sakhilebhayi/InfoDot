InfoDot — Business Directory & Business Discovery Marketplace
1. The Vision
Let's introduce a dedicated Businesses / Companies section on InfoDot.
The purpose is to create a central place where users can discover, explore and connect with businesses that have registered on InfoDot.
InfoDot should not only help businesses manage their operations and access the Dot ecosystem. It should also help businesses become discoverable to other people and organisations.
A user looking for a particular service should be able to visit InfoDot, browse registered businesses, explore their profiles, understand what they offer and contact them directly.
For businesses, this creates an opportunity to gain visibility, attract customers, establish credibility and develop commercial relationships.
The objective is to make InfoDot a trusted business discovery and connection platform.
This should feel like a professionally designed business marketplace, not a basic database listing companies.
2. Businesses / Companies Directory
Create a dedicated navigation item called Businesses or Companies.
This page should display businesses that have registered on InfoDot and have chosen to make their profiles discoverable.
Users should be able to browse companies without needing to know their names beforehand.
Business directory features
Include:

* Search businesses by name.
* Search by industry.
* Search by services offered.
* Search by business category.
* Filter by location.
* Filter by operating region.
* Filter by business size, where voluntarily provided.
* Filter by verification status.
* Filter by business type.
* Browse featured businesses.
* Browse recently registered businesses.
* Browse popular business categories.
* Browse businesses offering specific services.

Support pagination or efficient progressive loading for large directories.
Business categories
Create a flexible business categorisation system.
Examples include:

* Mining and natural resources.
* Construction and engineering.
* Technology and software.
* Professional services.
* Finance and accounting.
* Transport and logistics.
* Manufacturing.
* Agriculture and farming.
* Retail and wholesale.
* Healthcare.
* Education and training.
* Hospitality and tourism.
* Marketing and communications.
* Legal services.
* Security.
* Property and real estate.
* Consulting.
* Maintenance and technical services.
* Other business services.

Businesses should be able to select multiple relevant categories and describe their specific services.
Categories must be managed centrally so the directory remains consistent and searchable.
3. Business Discovery Experience
The directory should make finding businesses enjoyable and effortless.
Search experience
Create a prominent, intelligent search experience that allows users to search naturally.
Examples:

* "Mining equipment suppliers in Mpumalanga."
* "Accounting firms in Johannesburg."
* "Web development companies."
* "Companies offering electrical maintenance."
* "Construction businesses near Durban."

Search should consider business names, descriptions, industries, services and available location information.
If AI-assisted search is implemented, it must use actual business profile data and return explainable, relevant results.
Do not fabricate businesses or services.
Business listing presentation
Each business listing should present useful information at a glance:

* Business logo.
* Business name.
* Short description.
* Industry.
* Primary services.
* Location.
* Verification badge, where applicable.
* Website, where publicly provided.
* A clear link to view the full profile.

Design the listings to look professional and distinctive.
Avoid generic marketplace cards and cluttered layouts.
Users should be able to switch between suitable directory layouts, such as grid and list views, if this improves usability.
4. Individual Business Profile Pages
Every discoverable business should have its own professional public profile page.
This page should serve as the business's digital presence within InfoDot.
Business identity
Display:

* Business logo.
* Cover image.
* Registered business name.
* Trading name.
* Business description.
* Verification status.
* Industry and categories.
* Registration jurisdiction, where appropriate.
* Website.
* Year established, if provided.
* Operating locations.
* Service areas.

Products and services
Allow businesses to showcase what they offer.
Include:

* Services.
* Products.
* Service descriptions.
* Product or service images.
* Relevant pricing information, where the business chooses to publish it.
* Service locations.
* Business specialisations.
* Portfolio or project examples.
* Supporting documents or brochures where appropriate.

A business should be able to present itself professionally without needing to build an external website.
Business information
Provide structured sections for:

* About the company.
* Services and products.
* Industries served.
* Locations.
* Gallery.
* Portfolio.
* Business contact information.
* Website and social links.
* Relevant business announcements.

Only show information that the business has deliberately made public.
5. Contact and Communication
This is a core requirement.
Users must be able to contact businesses directly from their InfoDot profiles.
Provide two primary contact methods.
A. Contact through InfoDot Inbox
Introduce a prominent Message Business action on each business profile.
When a user selects it:

1. Open a conversation with the business through InfoDot Inbox.
2. Associate the conversation with the business identity, not merely an individual employee.
3. Allow the user to explain their enquiry.
4. Allow the business's authorised team members to receive and respond.
5. Maintain conversation history.
6. Provide unread indicators and notifications.
7. Allow attachments where supported and secure.
8. Preserve tenant and business-level permissions.

Businesses should be able to manage incoming enquiries through a dedicated business inbox.
Where appropriate, allow administrators to assign conversations to specific team members.
A conversation must not expose private internal business discussions or other customer conversations.
B. Public business contact details
Allow businesses to choose which contact details are displayed on their public profiles.
Supported fields should include:

* Business telephone number.
* Business email address.
* Website.
* Physical business address, where appropriate.
* Business social media links.
* Other approved public contact channels.

Provide clear actions such as:

* Call business.
* Email business.
* Visit website.
* Get directions, where an address is available.
* Message through InfoDot.

Public contact information must be controlled by the business.
Never expose private account information, personal phone numbers or internal employee contact details automatically.
Enquiry management
Give businesses a way to manage enquiries generated through their public profiles.
Consider:

* Enquiry inbox.
* Enquiry categories.
* Assignment to team members.
* Enquiry status.
* Internal notes.
* Response tracking.
* Follow-up reminders.
* Enquiry history.
* Basic enquiry analytics.

Possible enquiry statuses:

* New.
* Open.
* In progress.
* Awaiting response.
* Resolved.
* Closed.

6. Business Profile Management
Businesses must have control over their public presence.
Create a dedicated business profile management area where authorised users can:

* Edit business information.
* Upload or change logos.
* Upload or change cover images.
* Add products and services.
* Manage categories.
* Add operating locations.
* Update contact details.
* Manage website and social links.
* Add gallery images.
* Manage portfolios.
* Preview their public profile.
* Control profile visibility.
* Manage which information is public.
* Manage authorised profile administrators.

Changes to sensitive verified identity information should follow appropriate verification and review procedures.
Preserve the distinction between registered business identity and editable marketing information.
For example, a business must not be able to change its registered identity to another company's identity simply by editing its profile.
7. Business Verification and Trust
Integrate with InfoDot's business verification capabilities.
Display verification status accurately.
Support distinctions between:

* Unverified.
* Verification pending.
* Verified.
* Rejected.
* Suspended.
* Revoked.

A verified badge should link to an explanation of what was verified and what it means.
Do not imply that verification guarantees the quality of a business, its services or its commercial conduct.
Allow users to report:

* Impersonation.
* Incorrect business information.
* Suspicious business profiles.
* Misleading services.
* Fraudulent activity.
* Inappropriate content.

Create an administrative review workflow for reports.
Do not automatically label a business fraudulent simply because someone reports it.
8. Business Discovery Features Worth Building
Do not limit the directory to static listings.
Explore features that make InfoDot a genuinely useful place to discover businesses.
Featured businesses
Allow eligible businesses to feature their profiles.
Provide transparent rules for featured placements so paid visibility is not confused with verification or quality.
Similar businesses
Recommend businesses offering related services or operating in similar categories.
Business locations
Where reliable location data is available, allow users to explore businesses geographically.
Business collections
Allow users to save businesses to collections, such as:

* Suppliers.
* Potential partners.
* Service providers.
* Contractors.
* Businesses to contact later.

Business enquiries
Allow users to submit structured enquiries to businesses.
For example, someone looking for a construction contractor should be able to provide their requirements and contact the relevant business.
Business opportunities
Consider future capabilities for:

* Supplier discovery.
* Partnership enquiries.
* Business-to-business networking.
* Procurement opportunities.
* Service requests.
* Request-for-quotation workflows.
* Business collaboration.

These should be developed as genuine workflows rather than decorative buttons.
9. Business Analytics
Give businesses meaningful insight into their presence on InfoDot.
Provide analytics such as:

* Profile views.
* Search appearances.
* Search terms that led to their profile, where privacy permits.
* Message enquiries received.
* Contact actions.
* Website visits through the profile.
* Popular services.
* Enquiry response times.
* Profile engagement trends.

Only show analytics supported by actual recorded events.
Avoid fabricated statistics or misleading engagement metrics.
Businesses should be able to understand whether their InfoDot presence is helping them attract attention and generate enquiries.
10. UX/UI — Create a Beautiful Business Discovery Experience
The directory must feel like a natural part of InfoDot.
Use all relevant installed design skills, including:

* Product design.
* UX research.
* UI/UX design.
* Visual design.
* Branding.
* Typography.
* Information architecture.
* Interaction design.
* Responsive design.
* Accessibility.
* Motion design.
* UX writing.

Read and apply the relevant skills before implementation.
Design objectives
The experience should be:

* Beautiful.
* Professional.
* Human.
* Approachable.
* Easy to navigate.
* Visually distinctive.
* Fast.
* Responsive.
* Trustworthy.

Avoid generic AI-generated marketplace layouts, excessive cards, unnecessary gradients, oversized typography and cluttered information.
The directory should make discovering businesses feel natural and engaging.
Give the business profile page enough visual flexibility to showcase different kinds of businesses.
A technology company, construction contractor, accounting firm and mining supplier should all be able to present themselves professionally without being forced into an unsuitable template.
Responsive experience
Ensure the directory and profile pages work beautifully on desktop, tablet and mobile.
Contact actions must be particularly easy to use on mobile devices.
11. Privacy, Permissions and Security
Business discovery must respect business privacy and user permissions.
Implement:

* Business-controlled profile visibility.
* Public versus private information boundaries.
* Tenant isolation.
* Authorised business profile management.
* Role-based access.
* Secure Inbox conversations.
* Rate limiting.
* Spam protection.
* Enquiry abuse prevention.
* Safe file uploads.
* Audit logging.
* Secure public profile routes.
* Protection against unauthorised information disclosure.

A business must be able to choose whether its profile is:

* Publicly discoverable.
* Discoverable only to registered InfoDot users.
* Private and accessible only to authorised business members.

Respect these settings consistently in directory search, public URLs, recommendations and APIs.
Do not expose businesses that have opted out of discovery.
12. Technical Implementation
Use Laravel Boost throughout the development process.
Before making changes, inspect the existing InfoDot application, architecture and database.
Review:

* Existing business models.
* Business registration and verification.
* User and business relationships.
* Authentication and authorisation.
* InfoDot Inbox.
* Existing profile functionality.
* Search infrastructure.
* File and image storage.
* Notification services.
* Existing APIs.
* Existing tests.

Reuse existing capabilities wherever appropriate.
Do not introduce duplicate business registration systems, duplicate identities or competing messaging systems.
Build on the existing InfoDot business identity and Inbox architecture.
Use reusable services, appropriate database relationships, efficient search, tenant-aware queries and secure API endpoints.
Optimise the directory for growth as the number of registered businesses increases.
Do not use fake businesses, mock listings or simulated enquiry activity in production.
Every visible interaction must perform its actual intended function.
13. Testing and Acceptance Criteria
Test the entire experience end to end.
Business discovery

* A registered business enables public discoverability.
* The business appears in the directory.
* A user searches by business name.
* A user searches by industry.
* A user searches by service.
* A user filters by location.
* A user opens the business profile.
* A private business does not appear in discovery.

Business profile

* An authorised business administrator edits the profile.
* A business uploads a logo and cover image.
* A business adds services.
* A business updates public contact details.
* A user sees only information the business has made public.

Contact

* A user starts an InfoDot Inbox conversation with a business.
* An authorised business team member receives the enquiry.
* The business responds.
* Notifications are delivered.
* Conversation permissions are enforced.
* Public telephone and email actions work correctly.

Security

* Business A cannot edit Business B's profile.
* An ordinary user cannot access private business information.
* An unauthorised user cannot manage enquiries.
* Inbox conversations remain isolated.
* Hidden businesses cannot be discovered through search or direct APIs.

Test responsive layouts, accessibility, performance, error handling and existing functionality.
14. Final Implementation Instructions
Approach this as a core InfoDot product feature, not a small addition to the navigation.
The goal is to make InfoDot a place where businesses can establish their digital presence and where people can discover businesses they may want to work with, purchase from, partner with or contact.
Start by auditing the existing application and identifying the best way to integrate this feature into the current architecture.
Then implement the complete experience.
Do not stop at a directory page or attractive profile mockups.
Deliver a functioning business discovery experience with real business data, working search and filters, professional business profiles, secure contact through InfoDot Inbox, and business-controlled public contact information.
At completion, provide a report covering:

* Architecture reviewed.
* Features implemented.
* Database changes.
* UI/UX improvements.
* Inbox integration.
* Privacy and security controls.
* Tests performed and results.
* Outstanding issues.
* Recommended future enhancements.

The final experience should make InfoDot a destination where people discover and connect with businesses, not simply a place where businesses register their details.
