# INFODOT — CREDIBLE BUSINESS SOLUTIONS RESEARCH & EXPANSION

## Objective
Significantly expand the InfoDot Business Solutions library by researching credible, practical and specific solutions that help people start, operate, improve, manage and grow businesses. Conduct extensive research across the internet for solutions that are genuinely useful, evidence-based and actionable. Not generic tips, motivational advice or surface-level recommendations: specific business problems and the proven solutions, methods, tools, frameworks and practical approaches that address them. InfoDot should be a trusted business knowledge platform for someone starting, running or managing a business.

## 1. Research credible sources
Prioritise: government business agencies and portals; universities/business schools/research institutions; World Bank, IFC, OECD, ILO, UNIDO; reputable business development organisations and industry associations; established consulting/professional firms; credible entrepreneurship programmes and incubators; verified case studies; reputable software vendors where their solutions address identifiable problems; industry publications and professional communities. Prefer sources with methodologies, implementation steps, evidence, measurable outcomes, templates, examples. Record original source, publication/update date, references. A popular blog post, AI-generated article or unsupported claim is not credible evidence.

## 2. Discover specific business solutions — full lifecycle
A. Starting (opportunity, market research/validation, business models, plans, registration/structure, licensing, startup costs, funding/grants/investors, pricing, first customers, MVP, testing ideas). B. Management & operations (daily ops, SOPs, teams, productivity, suppliers/procurement, inventory, assets, service delivery/CX, risk, waste reduction, automation, KPIs/reporting). C. Sales & marketing (strategy, lead qualification, acquisition systems, conversion, campaigns, digital presence, positioning, social/content, retention/loyalty, referrals, CRM, B2B/government sales). D. Finance (cash-flow forecasts, working capital, budgeting, margins/break-even, debt, controls, fraud prevention, tax, statements, collections, investment evaluation, profitability). E. Technology & AI (what to automate, AI productivity, affordable software, choosing accounting/CRM/HR/inventory systems, data-driven decisions, dashboards, protecting information, cybersecurity, digitalising processes, e-commerce/payments, fit to size and budget). F. HR & leadership (recruiting, performance, onboarding, conflict, leadership, culture, retention, training, workforce planning, remote teams). G. Legal & risk (registration/statutory, employment law, contracts, IP, data privacy, H&S, industry regulation, insurance, continuity/DR, risk management). H. Growth (new markets, new products, franchising, partnerships, larger customers, scalability, international, investment-readiness, succession/exit). I. Industry-specific: agriculture, mining/industrial, construction, retail/wholesale, transport/logistics, manufacturing, hospitality/tourism, food/restaurants, professional services, tech, healthcare, education, beauty/lifestyle, real estate, cleaning/facilities — unique challenges and solutions per industry.

## 3. Prioritise practical and actionable
Every solution answers: what problem; which businesses; why it occurs; the recommended solution; implementation; resources/tools/skills/budget; expected results; how to measure; common mistakes; credible supporting sources. Include where appropriate: step-by-step instructions, checklists/templates, example calculations, recommended tools, case studies, estimated costs labelled with source and date, relevant South African regulations/institutions/funding/support. Never present estimates as guaranteed outcomes.

## 4. South African relevance
CIPC registration; SARS compliance; B-BBEE where applicable; CIDB; government and private procurement; SEDA support; IDC and credible financing; grants and funding programmes; SA labour legislation; POPIA; industry licensing. Do not assume an international solution applies directly — state where local adaptation, professional advice or regulatory verification is required.

## 5. Research quality and verification
Verify each source exists and supports the recommendation; prefer primary sources; distinguish proven practice from emerging ideas and opinion; include dates and flag outdated information; cross-check legal/financial/regulatory information against official sources; never invent statistics, case studies, organisations, tools or references; no duplication of existing InfoDot solutions; consolidate overlaps; flag conflicts instead of silently choosing; identify where professional advice is required. Confidence levels: High (authoritative sources/strong evidence), Medium (credible but limited), Emerging (promising, limited validation).

## 6. InfoDot solution structure
Title; short description; problem addressed; target business type/industry; lifecycle stage (Starting/Operating/Improving/Growing/Expanding); category; why it matters; recommended approach; step-by-step implementation; tools/resources; estimated cost where verifiable; expected benefits; success measurements/KPIs; common mistakes and risks; SA applicability; related solutions; supporting references and links; confidence level; last verified date. Understandable without formal business education, deep enough for experienced owners.

## 7. Genuinely useful, specific
Not "Improve Cash Flow" but: 13-week rolling forecast; cash conversion cycle; credit-control process; minimum cash reserve; supplier terms. Not "Improve Marketing" but: acquisition funnel for a local service business; CAC; referral programme; marketing ROI; repeat buyers. Specific enough that an owner knows what to do next.

## 8. Integrate with the existing platform
Inspect the current implementation first: categories, existing solutions, database structure, search/filtering, user interaction, relation to questions/discussions/contributions, Dot.Brain/Memory/Dopemine integrations. Preserve the architecture; no separate competing library; add through the existing structure and conventions. Ensure duplicate detection, correct categorisation/tagging, searchability, working links, responsive presentation, verified-vs-community distinction, attribution and version history, administrative review before publishing. No mock data or placeholders to inflate the library.

## 9. Execution approach
Research batches, not hundreds of low-quality entries. Per batch: inspect library; identify gaps; research from credible sources; verify evidence; duplicate checks; prepare complete records; add via the platform; test search/categorisation/links/presentation; report with evidence. Prioritise: expensive/common problems; less uncertainty for starters; revenue/profit/productivity; risk reduction; affordable for small businesses; SA opportunity/compliance access; applicable without big consulting budgets.

## 10. Final outcome
InfoDot as one of the most useful practical business knowledge resources: a person with a specific challenge finds credible, detailed guidance. Start by auditing the existing library and identifying gaps; research deeply; expand with high-quality, verified solutions. Do not stop at findings: where repository and deployment access permit, implement and test approved additions on the existing platform; be transparent about what needs human approval or external access. The measure of success is how many real business problems InfoDot helps people solve.
