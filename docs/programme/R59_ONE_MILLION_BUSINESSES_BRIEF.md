# R-59 brief (owner, 2026-11-24): INFOdot: One Million Businesses, Global Growth and Distribution Strategy

Stored verbatim as given by the owner (the only change: the spaced dashes in the six phase headings in section 16 are colons, to keep the house rule of no dashes in written text). The audit and the phased plan are in `R59_AUDIT_AND_PLAN.md`.

## 1. Strategic Objective

We are introducing a major strategic growth initiative into InfoDot called **One Million Businesses**.

The objective is to transform InfoDot into a globally recognised business ecosystem that attracts, onboards, supports, connects and retains at least 1 million businesses.

This must not be implemented as a standalone marketing campaign. It must become an integrated growth strategy supported by the InfoDot platform, the entire Dot ecosystem, business referrals, strategic partnerships, organic discovery, institutional distribution and product-led growth.

The underlying business proposition is:

**One business. One connected ecosystem. Everything you need to start, manage and grow.**

Our commercial pricing ambition is R100 per registered user per month, with access to the eligible Dot ecosystem through one unified business subscription model.

The platform must support international expansion, multiple currencies, local payment methods and country-specific compliance without compromising the South African foundation.

Before implementing anything, inspect the existing InfoDot architecture, database, subscription system, business profiles, user management, referral mechanisms, rewards system, Dot ecosystem integrations and existing functionality.

Reuse and extend existing functionality wherever appropriate. Do not create duplicate systems or break existing workflows.

## 2. Establish the One Million Businesses Growth Centre

Create a dedicated administrative growth management module within InfoDot called:

**InfoDot Growth Centre**

This will serve as the central command centre for managing and monitoring the acquisition of one million businesses.

It must provide a comprehensive dashboard displaying:

- Total registered businesses.
- Verified businesses.
- Active businesses.
- Paying businesses.
- Businesses acquired this month.
- Businesses acquired this year.
- Monthly recurring revenue.
- Annual recurring revenue.
- Total registered users.
- Total paying users.
- Average users per business.
- Average revenue per business.
- Business activation rate.
- Monthly business retention.
- Business churn.
- Referral conversions.
- Partnership-generated registrations.
- Organic acquisition.
- Acquisition costs by channel.
- Conversion rates across the onboarding funnel.
- Geographical distribution of businesses.
- Platform adoption across the Dot ecosystem.

Include a live progress indicator showing progress towards 1 million businesses.

The dashboard must support filtering by:
- Country.
- Province or region.
- Acquisition channel.
- Partnership.
- Registration date.
- Business category.
- Subscription status.
- Business size.
- Platform adoption.

Administrators must be able to drill down into the underlying business records and acquisition performance.

All statistics must come from real application data. Never introduce fabricated statistics, demonstration businesses or hardcoded performance figures.

## 3. Build the InfoDot Business Referral Programme

Turn existing InfoDot users and businesses into an acquisition network.

Every eligible business profile must have a unique referral link and referral code.

Businesses should be able to invite:
- Customers.
- Suppliers.
- Business partners.
- Employees.
- Accountants.
- Consultants.
- Other entrepreneurs.
- Other businesses.

Implement a referral tracking system that records:

- Referring business or user.
- Referral code.
- Referred business.
- Registration date.
- Verification status.
- Activation status.
- First meaningful platform activity.
- Subscription status.
- Reward eligibility.
- Reward history.

Introduce configurable referral rewards.

Suggested initial reward structure:

| Verified and activated referrals | Reward |
|---|---|
| 5 businesses | 100 points |
| 20 businesses | 500 points |
| 100 businesses | 3,000 points |
| 500 businesses | 20,000 points |

These are proposed starting values and must be configurable by administrators.

Rewards must only be issued after predefined verification and activation conditions have been met.

Implement fraud prevention, duplicate detection, self-referral prevention, referral attribution rules and reward reversal mechanisms.

Rewards should integrate with the existing InfoDot points and profile rewards infrastructure.

Explore allowing rewards to be redeemed for eligible subscription credits, premium services, storage and other ecosystem benefits.

Do not create a pyramid scheme or pay users simply for recruiting other recruiters. Rewards must be based on genuine business acquisition and product adoption.

## 4. Build the Business Partner Programme

Create an institutional and professional partner acquisition system.

InfoDot must allow approved partners to introduce and manage business onboarding campaigns.

Target partners include:

- Accounting firms.
- Bookkeeping firms.
- Business consultants.
- Business development agencies.
- Business associations.
- Chambers of commerce.
- Entrepreneurship incubators.
- Universities.
- Banks.
- Telecommunications companies.
- Insurance companies.
- Government enterprise development programmes.
- Corporate supplier development programmes.
- Franchise networks.

Create a dedicated partner portal.

Each partner should have:

- Partner profile.
- Unique partner identifier.
- Referral links.
- Business onboarding capabilities.
- Bulk invitation capabilities.
- Referral and conversion analytics.
- Client business directory.
- Campaign tracking.
- Partnership performance reports.
- Applicable commission or reward information.
- Agreement and documentation management.

Approved partners must be able to introduce businesses without gaining unauthorised access to those businesses' confidential information.

Implement proper tenant isolation, consent management and role-based permissions.

Create administrative approval workflows for partner applications, agreements, commission structures and partnership termination.

Support institutional campaigns where one organisation can introduce thousands of businesses through controlled bulk onboarding.

## 5. Build the InfoDot Business Network

Introduce a professional business discovery network inside InfoDot.

This is not intended to become another social media platform.

Its purpose is to help businesses discover, connect, collaborate and create commercial opportunities.

Businesses should be able to:

- Create public business profiles.
- Discover other businesses.
- Search for suppliers and service providers.
- Find potential customers.
- Discover business partners.
- Explore business opportunities.
- Advertise their services.
- Showcase their products.
- Initiate business enquiries.
- Collaborate with other businesses.
- Participate in approved business development programmes.

Introduce relevant discovery categories, geographical filters, verification indicators and business search functionality.

Public business profiles should be search-engine friendly while protecting private business information.

Maintain InfoDot's professional identity. Avoid unnecessary social-media mechanics such as follower competitions, vanity likes and engagement farming.

Prioritise genuine business connections and commercial utility.

## 6. Introduce Business Discovery and Organic Acquisition

Make InfoDot a destination for people searching for practical business guidance.

Build on the existing InfoDot solution library and knowledge-sharing functionality.

Develop an organic acquisition strategy around real business questions, including:

- How to start a business.
- How to register a company.
- How to create a business plan.
- How to manage business finances.
- How to find customers.
- How to hire employees.
- How to manage business operations.
- How to grow a small business.
- How to find suppliers.
- How to improve business performance.

Create search-engine-optimised resources, guides, tutorials and practical business solutions.

Where appropriate, connect each resource to the relevant Dot platform.

Examples:

| Business need | Relevant Dot platform |
|---|---|
| Business intelligence | Dot.Analytics |
| Financial management | Dot.Finance |
| Human resources | Dot.HR |
| Project management | Dot.Projects |
| Document management | Dot.Files |
| Task management | Dot.Tasks |
| Business communication | Dot.Plug and relevant communication platforms |
| Business productivity | Relevant Dot productivity platforms |

Do not create thin, repetitive or AI-generated search content simply to manipulate rankings.

Every resource must provide genuine value and be reviewed for accuracy.

Implement internal linking, structured metadata, indexing controls, canonical URLs and analytics to measure organic business acquisition.

## 7. Improve Business Onboarding and Conversion

Create an intelligent onboarding experience that helps each new business discover the value of InfoDot.

After registration, guide users through:

1. Creating or joining a business.
2. Establishing the business profile.
3. Selecting their business category.
4. Identifying their business objectives.
5. Inviting team members.
6. Discovering relevant Dot platforms.
7. Completing essential business setup.
8. Exploring available business resources.

Introduce an intelligent onboarding assistant that recommends the most relevant platforms based on the business's needs.

Do not overwhelm new businesses by displaying every Dot platform without context.

Make the experience personalised according to the business's size, industry, stage and objectives.

Every onboarding step must have measurable completion and conversion events.

## 8. Introduce the R100 Per User Pricing Strategy

Implement a transparent, unified subscription model.

Proposed pricing:

- Business registration: Free.
- Basic InfoDot access: Free.
- Business profile: Free.
- Paid user subscription: R100 per user per month.
- Eligible Dot platform access: Included according to the subscription entitlement.
- Additional premium services: Optional.

A business with five paid users should pay R500 per month.

A business with twenty paid users should pay R2,000 per month.

The subscription system must support:

- Business-level subscriptions.
- User seat allocation.
- Adding and removing employees.
- Subscription upgrades and downgrades.
- Monthly and annual billing.
- Multiple currencies.
- International payment methods.
- Invoices and receipts.
- Payment history.
- Subscription renewal.
- Failed payment handling.
- Grace periods.
- Cancellation.
- Refund workflows.
- VAT and country-specific tax considerations.
- Usage-based limits for expensive services.

AI consumption, storage, third-party API costs and other variable infrastructure expenses must have sensible usage limits and transparent policies.

Do not promise unlimited consumption where it could make the R100 model commercially unsustainable.

All subscription entitlements must be enforced centrally across the Dot ecosystem.

A user should not need separate subscriptions for every eligible Dot platform.

## 15. Technical, Security and Implementation Requirements

Before implementation, audit the existing architecture and produce a detailed implementation plan.

Requirements:

- Reuse the existing InfoDot authentication and identity infrastructure.
- Preserve centralised Dot ecosystem authentication.
- Respect tenant isolation.
- Implement role-based access control.
- Enforce subscription entitlements centrally.
- Protect referral attribution against manipulation.
- Maintain auditable financial and reward transactions.
- Use database transactions for billing and reward operations.
- Implement appropriate API rate limits.
- Validate all user-submitted data.
- Protect against duplicate business records.
- Apply privacy-by-design principles.
- Maintain appropriate consent records.
- Ensure background jobs are reliable and idempotent.
- Implement monitoring and error reporting.

All new modules must be production-ready, documented, tested and maintainable.

## 17. Definition of Success

The initiative must ultimately enable InfoDot to:

1. Acquire one million genuine businesses.
2. Convert a sustainable percentage into active paying customers.
3. Offer affordable business infrastructure at R100 per paid user per month.
4. Make the Dot ecosystem valuable enough that businesses use multiple platforms.
5. Grow through referrals and institutional partnerships rather than relying exclusively on paid advertising.
6. Support businesses from startup stage through to enterprise scale.
7. Expand internationally without rebuilding the core ecosystem for every market.

The ultimate objective is to make InfoDot a recognised global destination for starting, managing, connecting and growing businesses.
